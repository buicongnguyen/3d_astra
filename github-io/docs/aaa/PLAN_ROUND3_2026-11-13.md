## 1. Evaluation of the owner's ideas, with better versions

### 1.1 "Low ground can't fire up until it climbs": mostly already in the game, but invisible

**Fact (`src/sim/vision.js`, since 0.13.0):** a unit below a plateau can't see the plateau top beyond
its rim unless a friendly unit stands up there, and combat needs sight (`src/sim/combat.js`). A unit
shot by something it can't see walks toward it. So attackers in a valley already climb the ramp
and fight only once they're on top. The damage rules add +20% firing down, −15% firing up and +2 m
reach from above (`balance.json` terrain).

**The weakness is that the player can't see any of this.** Nothing on screen says "they can't see
you". The improvements:

1. **Show the rule.**
   - Units on a rim that nobody below can see get a small "hidden from below" eye mark.
   - The first time it happens, a hint explains it.
   - The ramp gets a red "kill zone" stripe on the minimap and the ground.
2. **Spotters break it.** This is the main counter-play.
   - An enemy scout or flyer that sees the top lets everyone below shoot up.
   - So later waves bring spotters first, and the player learns to kill spotters early.
   - This one rule ties the valley, air units and the Watcher together.
3. **Artillery fires up only at what a friend sees.** Barrage and the Rocket battery follow the same
   rule, so long range is not a way around it.
4. **Optional, later: terraces.** The owner's "almost the same height" fits a switchback road with two
   steps (low, middle, top), each step hidden from the one below.
   - Today the engine knows only two heights (`terrain.level()` returns 0 or 1).
   - Terraces are engine work. Do them only if the first valley map feels flat.

### 1.2 River defence: good, with one important rule

**Fact:** water already blocks walking. The only ways across are the two bridges and the middle ford
(`CROSSINGS` in `src/terrain.js`). The ford doesn't slow units today.

Better version:
1. **The ford slows units and leaves them exposed** (−40% speed, no cover). Bridges are narrow
   (2–3 units abreast), so both are natural kill zones.
2. **Blow a bridge, rebuild it.**
   - The player can demolish a bridge (an engineer action, or a charge on a Defence pad). Enemies
     then take the longer way.
   - Choosing which crossing to close is the main decision of a river level.
3. **Never seal the route.** A tower-defence rule: some path must always be open. The enemy answers
   a closed river:
   - its engineers lay a **pontoon** after about 20 s at a blown bridge;
   - or the wave turns to the ford;
   - flyers ignore the river.
4. **Wet units:** a unit in the ford can't use abilities. This is cheap and readable.

### 1.3 Campaign and its own mode: yes, built once

Build one **Frontier Defence** rule set (`rules.mode: "defence"`). It has build pads, garrison
towers, waves on a route, lives, a payout for each kill, three upgrade tiers and the flame tier look.

- **The mode:** the title screen gets a third button, **Defence**, with two map families, Valley and
  River, each graded 1–3 stars.
- **The campaign:** 2–3 story missions use the same rules ("Hold the valley", "Hold the river").
  Players learn the mode in the story, then find more of it in the menu.
- Both use the same code, the same wave bot and the same budget test.

### 1.4 Music: the studio tracks are licence-safe but the wrong style

**Fact:** `3d_body_dancer/studio-lan/public/songs/` has six instrumentals (Delight, Inspire, Smooth,
Glow, Breeze, Night Drive), about 1.6 MB each.
- The owner made them on 2026-10-21 with ACE-Step 1.5 (MIT; the model card allows commercial use).
- Their `LICENCE.txt` records this.
- They're dance-pop, not a frontier war score.

**Better: make a small new set with the same ACE-Step pipeline**, in a fitting style:
- frame drums or taiko, low strings and a plucked guitar;
- optionally a soft đàn bầu-like lead as a Meridian signature;
- instrumental only, cut to whole bars, made to loop.

The owner picks the takes on a blind listen page, as for the studio songs.

**How it stays light:**

| Track | Length | Size (AAC ~64 kbps) | When it loads |
|---|---|---|---|
| Title theme | 60–90 s loop | ~0.6 MB | After the first tap on the title screen |
| Campaign map / briefing | 60 s loop | ~0.5 MB | The first time the campaign opens |
| Defence: between waves | 60 s loop | ~0.5 MB | The first time Defence opens |
| Victory / defeat stings | 5–8 s | ~0.1 MB | With the title theme |

- In matches the current in-code adaptive score stays (calm → tense → combat layers). It reacts
  to the fight, and no recording can do that.
- **Total under 2 MB, never part of the first download.** The service worker caches each track the
  first time it plays.
- A Settings choice: Music **Recorded + adaptive / Adaptive only / Off**.
- Optional: the studio's "Across the Mountains" instrumental can join the listen page as a
  campaign-map candidate. The other studio tracks don't fit.

### 1.5 Air units: make them in our Blender kit, not downloads

**Fact:** every unit model is generated by our own kit (`tools/blender/frontier_kit.py`,
`generate_factions.py`). All 25 models together weigh 4.4 MB. The Wardens' Sentinel drone hovers,
but in the rules it's a ground unit.

**Better: generate the air units with the same kit**, so they:
- match the style;
- stay small (≤ 1,500 triangles, one material family);
- carry no licence question.

Free CC0 packs (Kenney, Poly Pizza) serve only as silhouette references. The owner approves review
sheets before wiring, as in Stage 2, then refines.

**Rules (the part that makes air worth having):**
- Flyers ignore terrain, water and walls, and **they see plateau tops** (spotters, 1.1).
- Only weapons marked `hits_air` can hit them:
  - yes: Rangers, anti-tank rockets, towers, the new flak upgrade;
  - no: melee, flame, tank shells, mortars.
- Low cap (at most 4 flyers each), with their own pop cost. Air supports the ground war; it doesn't
  replace it.
- New state is created lazily, so the sim hash (`d071360b`) is unchanged when nobody flies.

**First set (5 models):**

| Unit | Side | Job | Answer |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kite scout | Everyone (Barracks L2) | Cheap, unarmed; sees up cliffs and reveals cloaked units | Rangers, towers |
| Hawk gunship | Meridian (Foundry L3) | Strafes infantry | Flak, anti-tank |
| Ember balloon | Ashfall | Slow bomber with splash; drifts with the wind | Anything that hits air; it's fragile |
| Swift glider | Sunward | Fast raider that hits workers | Towers near the harvest |
| Flak upgrade | Tower research | The tower hits air (+50% against flyers) | — |

Later, after the owner refines the look: the Wardens' drone becomes a real flyer, and the Veil
gets a cloaked flyer.

**Drawing:** instanced like the others. Each flyer gets a blob shadow on the ground, which also
shows the player where it is, and a gentle bob. No new shadow casters.

### 1.6 More campaigns and different endings

**Facts:**
- One campaign, played as Meridian, 12 missions.
- Ashfall appears in all 12; Wardens and Veil appear only in missions 10–12.
- Stage 4 already designs three Commander decisions and two endings (`STAGE_4_STORY.md`,
  packages 4.4–4.6). None of it is built yet.

**Better structure:** one main campaign with real choices, plus short **Faction Chronicles**.
Four full campaigns (48 missions) would be too much to make well.

1. **Main campaign, "The Meridian Expedition": 12 → 15 missions in three acts.**
   - Rework the weak missions.
   - Add 3 new ones: *Hold the River* (defence rules), *Hold the Valley* (defence rules) and
     *Wings over Copper* (the first air raid).
   - Three decisions (missions 3, 5 and 10) set flags.
   - **Two endings:** the last mission has two versions, depending on who stands with you.
     - **"The Open Frontier":** you shared the Keystone; the factions sign a truce.
     - **"The Meridian Line":** you kept it; you hold the frontier alone.
   - Neither is the good or the bad ending. The ending cards are voiced by Vale, Sol, Hale, Maren
     and ECHO.
2. **Faction Chronicles: four short campaigns, 4 missions each,** played *as* that faction.
   - They unlock after Act 1 of the main campaign, and need "choose your army" (2.1) first.
   - Each tells the same war from the other side and reuses maps, so the cost goes into story,
     not art.

| Chronicle | Hero | Its four missions teach | Last mission |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ashfall: *Salvage Rights* | an Ashfall boss | Rush, salvage, Ember balloons | Defence: hold the scrapyard valley |
| Sunward: *The Long Road* | Sol | Raids, roads, gliders | The Three-Way Dunes from Sol's side |
| Wardens: *ECHO's Key* | Hale | Turtling, self-repair, shields | Defence: hold the river against Meridian |
| Veil: *The Quiet Survey* | a Veil surveyor | Cloak, far sight, no base fights | The dampers of Obsidian Storm, seen from inside |

3. **Endings that connect:**
   - Finishing a chronicle adds one line, and sometimes one ally squad, to the main finale.
     For example, Sunward done means Sol's riders join the last battle.
   - Replaying with other choices can reach the other ending.
   - The campaign map marks which endings you've seen.

**Mission count after the plan:** 15 main + 16 chronicle missions + 6 Defence levels (+ endless and
daily). That's 31 story missions, against 12 today.

### Speed rules (unchanged, checked in every release)

- **Phone battle draw calls:** at most 10% above today's numbers.
- **Defence:** a 120-enemy wave costs at most 113 draws.
- **First download:** the code grows by at most 10%. Modes, models and music load when first used.
- **Effects:** come only from capped pools that follow the governor.
- **Measuring:** count draws; fps on this shared machine is noise.

- Endings: two for the main campaign; chronicles add to them rather than having their own endings.
- Each release goes through the full local browser matrix and CI before it's pushed. One push per
