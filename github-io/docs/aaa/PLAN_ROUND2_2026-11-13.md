# Evaluation and round 2 plan, 2026-11-13 (at 0.15.1)

Asked by the owner: evaluate the game again, find the weaknesses, and plan how gameplay, story and
visual effects reach the AAA bar while the game stays fast; evaluate a tower-defence side game that
reuses the units (enemy troops walk a valley route, the player places cannons and units along it),
with upgrades and a more beautiful UI ("flame or something like that").

## 1. Where the game stands (0.15.1)

Overall about **7/10**: a polished, well-tested browser RTS. Round 1 (0.14.0–0.15.1) fixed what a
player sees first: readable alerts, visible high ground, crossings that read as crossings, bigger
explosions, layered sound, an AI that gathers and retreats, and a research panel that explains itself.

| Area | Score | Strong | Weak |
|---|---|---|---|
| Core rules | 7.5 | 16 units, abilities, ranks, counters, high ground, research branches | Tech is shallow: one research choice per match; no per-unit weapon choice; no air |
| Factions | 6.5 | Five rosters with their own units, building and trait | The player can only play Meridian; the four others are opponents only |
| Enemy AI | 7 | Gathers, weighs seen defences, retreats, focuses targets, splits | Faction round robin met for 4 of 10 pairs; Mirror without capture points may be too hard |
| Battlefield | 6.5 | High ground on 3 maps, rivers with bridges and a ford, walls | 4 of 7 maps flat; no cover, no weather, no day/night |
| Missions | 7 | 12 missions, 9 objective kinds, 3 difficulties, medals | 5 maps used twice; Ashfall in all 12; missions do not use height or rivers as goals; no rescue or no-base mission |
| Story delivery | 4 | Written arc, cast, portraits, Vietnamese | Text cards only: no staged scenes, no choices, nothing carries over, no campaign map |
| Visual effects | 7 | Fireballs, debris, scorch, burning buildings, hit flash, wrecks | Infantry deaths are plain; projectiles look alike across factions; no weather or time of day; no "hero moment" for abilities |
| Sound | ? | Layered, in code, mixed and capped | Nobody has listened yet |
| Interface | 7.5 | Checked at 3 sizes, readable states, phone-first | Functional rather than memorable; no visual language for tiers or rarity |
| Speed | 8.5 | Instanced units, draw budgets enforced in tests, frame governor | Not measured on a real phone; first download ~1.3 MB code + 4.4 MB models |

**The biggest gaps, in order:**
1. **Nobody has played it.** Every balance number comes from bots, and nobody has listened to the sound.
2. **Story delivery.** The writing exists; nothing is staged.
3. **Depth after the first hour.** One research choice, one playable army, flat tech.
4. **Signature moments.** No ability or event makes a player say "wow".

## 2. Evaluation of the tower-defence idea

**Verdict: a good idea, and a strong fit for this engine.** Build it as a separate mode, after
the gameplay depth work and alongside the story staging, not instead of them.

Why it fits:
- **Most of it already exists.** The game already has:
  - every unit model, with instanced drawing for crowds;
  - towers with levels and the combat rules;
  - scripted waves (the mission engine already "sends the waves");
  - deterministic replays and saves;
  - the high ground and the effects;
  - the message and hint system.
- **The five factions make natural enemy types.** Each one teaches a counter from the main game:

| Enemy | Its job in a wave | What beats it |
|---|---|---|
| Scrappers | Swarms | Splash |
| Riders | Fast runners | Slows |
| Walkers | Armour | Anti-tank |
| Shades | Cloaked | Spotters (Watcher) |
| Wagons | Heal the wave | Focus fire |
| Drones | Carry shields | Fire that breaks shields |

- **Phones.** Short 5–10 minute levels and tap-to-place suit phones better than full RTS control.
- **Speed.** Enemies follow a fixed route, so the mode needs no pathfinding and suits the instanced batches.

Risks:
- It splits attention. A half-finished second mode hurts more than it helps.
- Tower defence needs its own camera, a full-valley overview that works in portrait.
- It needs its own placement UI: build pads and an upgrade sheet.
- It needs at least 6 tower roles to be interesting.
- It needs its own balance tool: a wave bot that plays it like the mission checker does.

Improvements to the idea:
1. **Fixed build pads along the valley, not free placement (Kingdom Rush style).** Pads are readable, easy on phones and easy to balance. Put some pads on plateaus, where the high-ground bonus applies, and some by bridges and fords.
2. **Garrison towers made from the army's own units.** The player builds an emplacement, then stations a unit in it, and that unit becomes the tower's weapon. Players already know the units, and no new models are needed.

| Garrison | Role |
|---|---|
| Ranger nest | Single target |
| Breaker mortar | Splash, can use Barrage |
| Anti-tank post | Armour |
| Battle tank emplacement | Heavy |
| Scorcher flame trap | Burns a line |
| Watcher post | Reveals Shades, marks targets |

3. **Three tiers per tower, with a branch at tier 3.** For example:
   - Breaker: *Cluster shells* (wider blast) or *Guided rounds* (longer range).
   - Ranger: *Marksman* or *Suppressor* (slows the target).

   These branches double as tests for the weapon variants planned for the main game (section 3, A2).
4. **A commander or hero on the field.** Hale or Sol, moved by tap, with two abilities: Barrage, and reinforcements that drop three Vanguards to block the road.
5. **Stars and meta upgrades.** Three stars per level by lives kept. Stars buy permanent perks: cheaper first tower, a faster Barrage, an extra life.
6. **Story tie-in.** Each level is a "valley defence" side operation unlocked on the campaign map between missions. That gives the campaign map a second purpose.
7. **Replay.** An endless mode and a daily seed with a local best score, both cheap because the simulation is deterministic.

### The "flame" UI idea

Good: tiers and upgrades need a visual language. The flames must cost almost nothing, so:
- **Tier frames:** bronze, steel and gold trims on tower and research tiles, and the same tier tint on the units themselves through the per-instance colour that already exists.
- **Fire accents:**
  - a short ember burst (a few CSS particles, opacity and transform only) when an upgrade becomes affordable or completes;
  - a slow heat glow on the frame of a tier-3 or "done" tile;
  - one shared sprite sheet for flame edges, no video;
  - all of it off under reduced motion and on the Eco quality setting.
- **No full-screen animated backgrounds** on phones, and no blur filters on large elements.
- **Keep the house rules:** text 11 px or larger, taps 44 px or larger, no sideways scroll.

### Speed rules for every item (the game must stay fast)

- **Phone battle draw calls:** at most 10% above today's test numbers (battle-eco 107, three-factions-eco 169). The render-budget and boundary-fx suites enforce this.
- **Tower defence:** a 120-enemy wave on the phone view costs no more than today's 60-unit battle (113 draws). A new tdefence-budget suite will enforce this.
- **First download:** the code may grow by at most 10%. New modes and models load only when first opened.
- **Effects:** come only from fixed pools with caps. They scale down with the frame governor and with reduced motion.
- **Measuring:** count draw calls; fps numbers on this shared machine are noise.
